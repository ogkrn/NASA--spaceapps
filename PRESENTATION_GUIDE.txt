ARES-LUNA — evidence and exploration scenarios
Presentation guide aligned with Package A rebased on e1d0aaa

1. THE PROBLEM IN SIMPLE WORDS
Earth environments can help test selected aspects of Moon and Mars exploration.
Visual resemblance alone is not enough. A useful analog must suit a specific
experiment, and its differences from the planetary environment must be explained.

2. OUR SOLUTION
An interactive prototype connecting descriptions of known Earth analogs with
Moon/Mars scenario comparisons and a daily resource-budget exercise. We separate
source-supported descriptions from unverified engineering assumptions.

3. HOW A USER USES THE PROJECT
Choose Moon or Mars, select a site card or globe marker, read the source evidence,
compare Earth analogs, adjust ranking priorities, and run a resource-budget scenario.
Inspect deficits and assumptions, not just the headline indicator. Use educational
mode and the deterministic briefing for accessible explanations.

4. WHAT WE BUILT
Nine planetary scenarios, six known Earth analogs, a six-priority TOPSIS ranking,
a subjective analog-profile shortlist, three power modes, water/oxygen/power
accounting, daily telemetry, source-linked briefings, lessons and a quiz.
The globe uses external visual textures when available and a procedural fallback.
Its lighting and orbital tracks are illustrative, not live mission telemetry.
A calculation service failure is reported explicitly; results are not invented.

5. NASA AND SPACE-AGENCY RESOURCES
Claim-level NASA, ESA and USGS links support descriptive site context. The separate
resource registry lists archives useful for future measurement extraction. An
archive link does not validate a numerical value. No NASA product download or
measurement extraction is implemented. The catalog script checks record structure
and emits a manifest with empty product identifiers; it is not scientific validation.

6. TECHNOLOGY USED
Next.js, React, TypeScript and Three.js frontend; FastAPI, Pydantic and NumPy backend;
local JSON catalogs. No paid API, trained model, generative service or database is
required. Dependency installation, evidence links and optional external textures
require Internet access; the running backend computes from local catalogs.

7. WHAT THE PROJECT ACCOMPLISHES AGAINST THE CHALLENGE
It communicates known analog contexts, makes comparison assumptions visible and
connects exploration topics to testing activities. It does not yet discover new
Earth sites or perform a global search using comparable satellite measurements.
That gap matters and should be acknowledged during judging.

8. SIMPLE DEMO SCRIPT
- Select a Moon scenario and open the descriptive source links.
- Open Analog Discovery and explain one Earth location's field-testing context.
- Explain that the /100 index is subjective, not a physical similarity percentage.
- Change a ranking priority to demonstrate the effect of user preferences.
- Run a simulator scenario, change an input and show the rerun warning.
- Inspect full telemetry, water/oxygen/power deficits and the first failure day.
- Switch to Mars and show its different descriptive context and source links.
- Finish with the observation-extraction and validation work still required.

9. QUESTIONS JUDGES MAY ASK
Are the engineering inputs NASA measurements?
No. They are illustrative assumptions inherited from the prototype. Source links
support specific descriptive claims, not every number displayed alongside them.

Does NOMINAL mean humans would survive?
No. It means the declared simplified daily budget met the modeled demands and
reserve rules. Food, equipment reliability, intraday power and real radiation
transport are not validated. The indicator is not a survival probability.

Is the analog similarity scientifically calibrated?
No. The retained rule compares subjective profiles and non-equivalent proxy
features. We disclose this limitation and do not call it measured physical fidelity.

Why use Earth if conditions are different?
An Earth site can support a specific terrain, geology or operations experiment
without reproducing reduced gravity, vacuum, Mars pressure or space radiation.

Does the globe show live data?
No. It is a visual navigation aid with fixed lighting and decorative tracks.
External texture availability is not guaranteed; cards work without WebGL.

10. NEXT VERSION PLAN
Extract comparable observations with product/version IDs, footprints, units,
resolution, time coverage and uncertainty. Keep measured observations separate
from scenario inputs. Calibrate matching on equivalent physical dimensions,
validate against independent comparisons, then broaden the search beyond known sites.

11. ONE-SENTENCE PROJECT SUMMARY
ARES-LUNA links evidence about known terrestrial analogs with transparent planetary
comparison scenarios and a resource-budget demonstrator, while showing where
measurements and scientific validation are still missing.
