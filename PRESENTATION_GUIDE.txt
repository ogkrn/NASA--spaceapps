ARES-LUNA
Planetary Analog Intelligence and Mission Simulation
NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 Presentation Guide


1. THE PROBLEM IN SIMPLE WORDS

NASA wants to build permanent human bases on the Moon and eventually send people to Mars.
Before sending astronauts, NASA must test rovers, spacesuits, habitats, drills, scientific
instruments, navigation systems, and life-support technology.

Testing directly on the Moon or Mars is expensive, dangerous, and difficult. Earth can be
used as a testing laboratory, but only if we choose Earth locations that are scientifically
similar to the target environment.

A place can look like Mars and still be a poor analog. A desert may have the wrong minerals,
temperature range, terrain, radiation conditions, ice conditions, or soil behavior.

The challenge is therefore:

Find and compare Earth locations that can help test lunar and Martian technologies, while
also helping scientists evaluate possible Moon and Mars settlement locations.


2. OUR SOLUTION

ARES-LUNA is an interactive decision-support prototype for planetary settlement planning.

It answers three practical questions:

1. Which lunar or Martian locations deserve attention?
2. Which Earth environments are useful for testing technology for those locations?
3. Would a proposed crew, power system, water loop, and habitat plan remain viable?

The important idea is that ARES-LUNA does not say that Earth and another planet are identical.
It shows a transparent, assumption-based comparison and explains what still needs to be
validated with real mission products.


3. HOW A USER USES THE PROJECT

Step 1: Choose the Moon or Mars.

Step 2: Explore candidate landing and settlement locations on the interactive 3D globe.

Step 3: Select a site and review the main decision factors:
        - sunlight or solar illumination
        - water-ice or subsurface volatile potential
        - slope and terrain trafficability
        - communication visibility
        - natural radiation shielding
        - thermal range

Step 4: Open Earth Analogs.

The analog engine ranks Earth environments using several proxy dimensions, such as:
        - topography
        - geological or regolith context
        - thermal range
        - volatile or ice relevance
        - isolation and logistics context

The result is a shortlist of places where NASA-style rover, drilling, sampling, mobility,
habitat, or field-science tests could be performed.

Step 5: Open the Mission Simulator.

The user changes:
        - mission duration
        - crew size
        - hybrid, solar, or nuclear power
        - battery capacity
        - water recycling efficiency
        - regolith excavation rate
        - habitat burial depth for radiation shielding

The simulator produces day-by-day telemetry for power, battery charge, water, oxygen,
radiation, and system stress. It also reports bottlenecks and recommendations.

Step 6: Use the explanation and education modes.

The project provides a plain-language evidence briefing, lessons about lunar and Martian
survival engineering, and a short knowledge quiz. This makes the scientific ideas easier
for judges, students, and non-specialists to understand.


4. WHAT WE BUILT

Candidate site explorer:
    A Moon and Mars catalogue with settlement-relevant environmental fields.

Multi-criteria ranking:
    A TOPSIS-style decision engine ranks sites according to user-selected weights.
    This lets a user decide whether sunlight, water, slope, communication, shielding, or
    thermal stability is most important.

Automated analog discovery:
    A vector-based screening engine compares target sites with Earth analogues and returns
    ranked matches plus a scientific rationale.

3D visualization:
    A Three.js WebGL globe displays planetary candidate locations and lets the user select
    a landing node.

Mission simulator:
    A Python/FastAPI engine models simplified power, consumables, water recycling, oxygen
    balance, radiation accumulation, battery behavior, and mission bottlenecks.

Evidence briefings:
    The application creates site-specific scientific, educational, and executive summaries.
    These are transparent application briefings, not claims of a live NASA-certified AI model.

Educational exploration:
    Interactive lessons explain sunlight, permanently shadowed regions, water, radiation,
    lava tubes, ISRU, and why Earth analogs matter.

Data provenance:
    The interface identifies the NASA resource families connected to each group of fields,
    shows confidence labels, and explains when values are catalogue screening values.

Offline demonstration mode:
    If the backend is unavailable, the interface can still demonstrate the product using
    embedded values. The UI labels this as offline demo data instead of silently presenting
    it as live scientific output.


5. NASA AND SPACE-AGENCY RESOURCES

The prototype uses the following official resource families as the scientific data
foundation and provenance references:

NASA Earthdata:
    Earth observation, terrain, climate, and analog-environment context.

NASA Planetary Data System (PDS):
    Long-term archive and provenance reference for planetary mission products.

Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) Diviner:
    Lunar surface temperature and thermal environment context, including permanently
    shadowed region analysis.

Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) LOLA:
    Lunar laser altimetry, elevation, terrain, and slope context.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) HiRISE:
    High-resolution Mars surface imagery and terrain context.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) CRISM:
    Mars mineralogical and spectral context.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) SHARAD:
    Mars subsurface radar context, including buried ice and subsurface layers.

JAXA SELENE/Kaguya:
    Supplementary lunar topography and surface context.

Earth analog and field campaign context includes:
    NASA Haughton-Mars Project on Devon Island
    HI-SEAS and volcanic environments in Hawaii
    Atacama Desert field testing
    AMASE in Svalbard
    ESA PANGAEA training in Lanzarote
    McMurdo Dry Valleys in Antarctica

Important honesty point:
The current prototype is a scientifically transparent catalogue and screening system.
It references the official NASA resource families, but it does not yet claim that every
displayed number was downloaded directly from a specific NASA product. Product identifiers,
retrieval timestamps, calibration metadata, and uncertainty ranges still need to be added
for operational scientific use.

7. WHAT THE PROJECT ACCOMPLISHES AGAINST THE CHALLENGE

Strongly addressed:
    - It explains why visual similarity alone is not enough.
    - It compares lunar and Martian candidate environments using multiple factors.
    - It identifies Earth environments that can support technology testing.
    - It connects analog environments to rover, drilling, sampling, habitat, and mobility work.
    - It makes the comparison understandable through a visual interface.
    - It includes NASA resource-family provenance rather than presenting unexplained numbers.
    - It includes a mission simulator showing the operational consequences of site choices.
    - It includes an educational mode for non-specialists and judges.
    - It exposes limitations instead of hiding uncertainty.

Partially addressed:
    - Advanced analog discovery is currently proxy-based and catalogue-driven.
    - The 3D terrain is a visualization layer, not yet a direct NASA terrain-data viewer.
    - The AI briefing is an evidence-based application explanation, not a connected
      generative AI service.
    - The simulator is a planning model, not a NASA flight-certified life-support model.
    - Several candidate sites are known or famous examples; finding overlooked locations
      requires larger product-level datasets and a discovery pipeline.

Not yet complete for operational science:
    - Direct ingestion of selected NASA product IDs.
    - Product retrieval dates and version information.
    - Calibrated uncertainty and confidence intervals.
    - Shared physical units for every analog-comparison dimension.
    - Real NASA-derived terrain, illumination, thermal, mineralogy, and subsurface layers
      rendered in the 3D globe.
    - Validation against field-test measurements and mission hardware datasets.

Conclusion:
    ARES-LUNA accomplishes the challenge as a strong educational and decision-support
    prototype. It demonstrates the complete workflow from planetary site screening to
    Earth analog selection to mission simulation. It should be presented as a transparent
    prototype for discovering and prioritizing analog sites, not as the final scientific
    authority for selecting a real NASA base.


8. SIMPLE DEMO SCRIPT FOR THE PRESENTATION

Opening:
    "Building a base on the Moon or Mars is not only a location problem. It is also a
    technology-testing problem. We need Earth environments that reproduce the important
    conditions, not just the appearance."

Show the Moon:
    Select Moon and point to Shackleton Crater Rim. Explain that sunlight, ice potential,
    slopes, temperature, communication, and shielding all affect settlement planning.

Show provenance:
    Open the data provenance panel. Point out the LRO Diviner and LOLA references. Explain
    that the project labels catalogue values and does not pretend that unverified values
    are final mission measurements.

Show analog discovery:
    Click "Find Earth Analogs." Explain that Devon Island or another ranked site is useful
    because it supports rover, drilling, field geology, or habitat testing. Emphasize that
    the percentage is a proxy screening score, not a claim that the locations are identical.

Show simulator:
    Click "Simulate Mission." Change crew size, duration, power system, or water recycling.
    Run the simulation and show power, battery, water, oxygen, radiation, and bottlenecks.

Show education mode:
    Open Educational Mode and use one lesson or quiz question. Explain that the same
    scientific reasoning is available to students and judges without requiring a
    planetary-science background.

Closing:
    "ARES-LUNA turns a difficult planetary science question into a transparent workflow:
    compare the target, find the right Earth testing ground, simulate the mission, and
    clearly show what is known, what is assumed, and what must be validated next."


9. QUESTIONS JUDGES MAY ASK

Q: Are the similarity percentages real measurements?
A:
    No. They are assumption-based proxy scores used to rank candidates during early
    screening. The interface says this clearly. Product-level calibration is the next step.

Q: Did you use NASA data?
A:
    The project is organized around NASA resource families including Earthdata, PDS, LRO
    Diviner, LRO LOLA, MRO HiRISE, MRO CRISM, and MRO SHARAD. The current prototype uses
    curated catalogue values linked to those resource families. We do not claim a value is
    a direct product measurement until its product ID and retrieval metadata are stored.

Q: Is the AI connected to a language model?
A:
    The current briefing is a transparent evidence-based explanation generated by the
    application. It is deliberately labelled as not being a live AI or NASA-certified
    recommendation. A future version can connect a model with retrieval-augmented NASA
    evidence and citations.

Q: Is the simulator flight-ready?
A:
    No. It is a planning and education simulator. It helps users understand trade-offs
    between power, water, oxygen, crew size, radiation shielding, and mission duration.
    Engineering qualification would require validated hardware models, uncertainty
    propagation, and mission-specific NASA constraints.

Q: Why are Earth analogs useful?
A:
    Earth analogs allow teams to test rovers, suits, drills, sampling methods, navigation,
    habitats, and field procedures before attempting the same work in space.


10. NEXT VERSION PLAN

Priority 1:
    Ingest a small, traceable set of actual NASA products for a few demonstration sites.
    Store product IDs, URLs, retrieval timestamps, processing level, and uncertainty.

Priority 2:
    Add a technology-testing matrix:
    analog site -> tested technology -> NASA or agency campaign -> measured result.

Priority 3:
    Replace the procedural globe layers with NASA-derived terrain, illumination, thermal,
    mineralogy, and subsurface overlays.

Priority 4:
    Add calibrated similarity methods with explicit units, normalization, missing-data
    handling, uncertainty propagation, and sensitivity analysis.

Priority 5:
    Expand the discovery search to less famous Earth locations so the system can identify
    overlooked analogs instead of only ranking known examples.

Priority 6:
    Improve accessibility, keyboard navigation, mobile navigation, modal focus handling,
    and reduced-motion support.


11. ONE-SENTENCE PROJECT SUMMARY

ARES-LUNA is a transparent NASA-resource-guided prototype that helps people compare Moon
and Mars settlement candidates, discover Earth environments for testing exploration
technology, simulate mission survival trade-offs, and understand the science in simple
language.
